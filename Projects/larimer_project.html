Larimer County, Colorado

Rrural Parcel Buildability Screening

Project Overview

Location: Larimer County, Colorado
Properties: Three O (Open) zoned rural parcels

This project presents a GIS-based preliminary feasibility screening of three rural parcels in Larimer County using publicly available datasets. The analysis evaluated terrain suitability, slope constraints, zoning considerations, conceptual building locations, and soil limitations from the USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) to support early-stage land evaluation before purchase.

Parcel Comparison

A slope-based comparison was used to evaluate the amount of lower-slope terrain available on each parcel. Lower-Slope Terrain generally more favorable for potential building locations, while steeper areas may require additional grading, engineering, or site investigation.

The comparison shows that all three parcels contain limited areas of gentler terrain. 1600 Whale Rock Rd has the highest percentage of lower-slope terrain, while 100 Canyon Gulch Ct provides the largest total amount of lower-slope area because of its larger size.

  • Cabin: 40 × 60 ft
  • Standard house: 60 × 80 ft
  • Large house with garage: 80 × 100 ft

Potential building locations are generally limited to small ridge benches and hilltop shoulders rather than large continuous flat areas.

Key Findings

  • Three rural O-zoned parcels were evaluated to compare terrain conditions and potential development opportunities.
  • Slope analysis showed that suitable building areas are limited and are generally located on smaller flatter areas such as ridge benches and hilltop sections.
  • Conceptual house locations were tested while considering slope conditions and 25 ft property setbacks.
  • 1600 Whale Rock Rd and 100 Canyon Gulch Ct contain areas that may be more suitable for potential residential development based on terrain conditions.
  • 813 Hernia Hill Tr (8.2 acres) requires additional verification because parcel size may affect future residential development eligibility.
  • NRCS soil information indicates potential septic limitations across the evaluated properties, requiring additional field investigation before construction.

Soil and Septic Considerations

Soil information was reviewed using the USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) Web Soil Survey, which provides mapped soil characteristics and information useful for evaluating potential limitations for septic systems and development.

Conclusion

GIS-based parcel screening can help identify promising building locations and potential development challenges before major investments are made. This type of early evaluation supports smarter land-purchase decisions and helps determine when additional professional studies are recommended.
